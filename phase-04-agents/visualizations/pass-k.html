pass@k vs pass^k: how reliable is your agent really?

If a task succeeds with probability p per attempt, pass@k (at least one of k attempts works) climbs towards 100%, while pass^k (all k attempts work) falls towards 0. Your customers each get one attempt, so pass^k is the one that matches what they experience.

Measured in Lab 06 (gemini-3.5-flash-lite, 5 tasks × 3 trials): run 1 15/15, run 2 14/15 → pass^3 1.00 then 0.80, on identical code. τ-bench reported pass^8 under 25% for strong models on retail tasks.
